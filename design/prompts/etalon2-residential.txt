================ КОПИРОВАТЬ ОТСЮДА ================

Match the colour density, prop density and rendering of the attached reference
images, but the subject is the Mars scene described below.

NON-NEGOTIABLE RENDER RULES. These override everything else in this prompt.
1. NO OUTLINES. No contour line, no ink line, no edge stroke, no dark rim on any
   object at any thickness. Shapes separate through colour and value only.
2. VOLUME, NOT FLAT FILL. Every surface carries a two-step gradient from a lighter
   lit facet into a darker shaded facet. No wall, roof or prop is a single flat tone.
3. NO CUBES, NO BOXES. Buildings are soft rounded volumes: barrel vaults, domes,
   capsules, cylinders with rounded caps, fat shells with very large corner radii.
   Nothing reads as a shipping crate or a rounded-off box.
4. EVERY BUILDING A DIFFERENT SILHOUETTE. Never the same shell repeated in another
   colour.
5. ONE KEY LIGHT from the upper left at 55 degrees above the horizon. Every object
   drops a soft blurred shadow offset toward the lower right, extending past its own
   footprint. No near-vertical light, no shadow hidden under its object.

Soft volumetric shading baked into a 2D mobile game asset, in the rendering
language of Township and Hay Day. Stylized toy-like light falloff, not physically
realistic lighting. Finish is semi-gloss painted metal and ceramic composite with a
soft sheen, the polished look of a premium mobile city-builder.

A three-quarter view at roughly 45 degrees on a residential quarter of a Mars colony,
moderate camera distance so individual buildings and props stay readable. The horizon
sits about a quarter of the way down from the top, with a band of pale rosy haze under
a peach-pale sky.

Six habitat modules, each a clearly different rounded shape, no two alike:
a brick red barrel-vault module, a half-cylinder lying on its side with rounded ends;
a mustard yellow two-storey shell with a domed roof cap and a curved outside stair;
a terracotta low capsule module with a curved porch canopy over its door;
a teal blue upright cylinder with a rounded cap and a ring walkway around it;
a sage green wide low shell with a soft rounded ridge running along its roof;
a cream small pill-shaped module with a rounded bay window.
Three of them form a tight row sharing the warm red-terracotta family, still separate
silhouettes with a visible gap between them; the rest stand apart. Every module has a
coloured lamp beside its door and a small numbered plate.

Behind the row on the right sits one hydroponic dome, a true hemisphere shaded with a
smooth radial gradient, its frame ribs painted a saturated green-teal, dense emerald
crop rows glowing through the glass as the most saturated green in the frame; a weather
mast on a tripod stands beside its entrance and painted tile rings its base.

A painted path crosses the foreground, edged on both sides by long planter troughs of
muted sage-green moss and lichen behind a low glass rim, with a partial transparent
canopy arching over one stretch of trough. A teal coolant tank with a level gauge
stands against the leftmost block, and a thin glazed ground pipe carrying visibly
bluish fluid runs from it toward the dome.

Props pressed against the buildings: a planter of bright succulents by a door, a line
of small route flags on a thin mast along the path, numbered cargo containers striped
in terracotta and cream stacked beside the cream block, a cable spool, a bench under
a striped fabric awning by the airlock. Four colonists in light pastel suits walk the
path and stand talking by a door.

Every building is a separate standalone structure spaced apart on open ground with
clear air and visible ground around it, never fused into a shared mass, never joined
by tube corridors or connecting walkways, linked only by thin ground-level pipes and
painted paths. Every structure is squat, height roughly equal to its own footprint
width, with large rounded corners and no sharp edges.

At least four colour families are visible at once and exactly one of them is cool
teal-cyan; no family except the sky covers more than 40% of the frame. Painted
structures and decorative props read clearly more saturated than the sky and the
ground behind them, by a wide and obvious margin. Pure clean blue never appears on
any world object.

Ground: warm ochre sand as the base, a paler trodden dust path, a rust-red dust band
at the far edge of the built area, painted tile at the dome entrance. Sky is peach-pale
Martian daytime graduating to muted blue-grey near the zenith, occupying only the top
quarter of the frame and never spilling below the horizon line.

Mood: sunny midday, a lived-in neighbourhood, calm optimism.

NEGATIVE:
any outline or contour line on world objects, thick black outline, comic ink line;
single flat color fill with no gradient anywhere on an object;
hard-edged two-tone shading with a sharp light/shadow cutoff;
boxy angular crate buildings, square-cornered domes, identical repeated modules;
monochrome beige blob, whole scene at one flat lightness, everything cream and ochre;
cream or sand-white hull on every building, unpainted residential blocks;
moss or plants growing loose in open ground with no glass rim or canopy;
pure clean blue on world objects;
yellow-and-black hazard hatching, warning stripes, alarm markings;
sky filling half the frame, sky spilling below the horizon line;
one single flat ground material across the whole frame;
near-vertical overhead light, shadow hidden directly under its object;
objects floating with no visible offset shadow;
flat 2D vector illustration, sticker-sheet look, infographic icon set, paper cutout;
buildings fused into one mass, joined by tube corridors, shared walls;
tall tower-like vertically stretched structures;
empty bare ground between buildings;
photorealistic lighting, long sunset shadows, dust storm, craters, cracks, ruins;
blue Earth sky, dark starry sky, text, UI overlay
